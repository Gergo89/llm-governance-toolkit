# REF Primitive Basis: A Crosswalk to the Foundational-Ontology and Metaphysics Literature

*Purpose: to locate the REF program's independently-derived positions within existing scholarship — so that, if the ontology itself is ever pursued as a contribution, it engages the literature it must, and so that its genuinely-recombinant moves are separated from its re-derivations. This is a positioning document, not an endorsement of REF's claims.*

**Citation caveat.** Attributions below are given from established knowledge of canonical works. Verify each against the primary source before using in a submission; do not cite page numbers from this document.

---

## The one-paragraph situation

REF independently converges on a **relation/difference/process-first structural ontology**: it takes *Difference, Relation, Transformation, Constraint* as primitive and derives Entity, Identity, State, Boundary, Persistence, Emergence as later, contextual constructions. This is a respectable, actively-defended position in the contemporary metaphysics of science — but it is *not new*, and REF cites none of the traditions that already occupy this ground. The work's defensible novelty, if any, is not the primitive choice; it is the **method** (deterministic generation + governance + explicit unresolved-dependency accounting — see the companion methodology note). The crosswalk below is what a reviewer in formal ontology or metaphysics of science would expect the work to engage.

## Concept-by-concept mapping

**Difference as ontological primitive; Distinction as its epistemic recognition.**
REF's sharpest move — separating ontological non-equivalence (*Difference*) from the observer's act of *Distinction* — is the founding gesture of George Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* (1969), where "draw a distinction" is the primitive operation. It also runs through Gregory Bateson's "a difference that makes a difference" (information as difference) and, in a different register, Gilles Deleuze's *Difference and Repetition* (1968), which argues difference is prior to identity. REF's insistence that recognition must never be "smuggled into what exists" is precisely the ontological/epistemic cut these authors thematize. *Gap to close: REF must position against Spencer-Brown at minimum; the "Difference before Distinction" claim is his.*

**Relation as primitive; Entity as derived.**
"Structure/relations are fundamental, objects are derivative" is **ontic structural realism** — James Ladyman ("What is Structural Realism?", 1998) and Ladyman & Ross, *Every Thing Must Go: Metaphysics Naturalized* (2007), with Steven French (*The Structure of the World*, 2014). The mathematical form of "objects as positions in a structure" is **category-theoretic structuralism** (F. W. Lawvere's categorical foundations; Steve Awodey on structuralism). Ernst Cassirer's *Substance and Function* (1910) is the classical ancestor (from thing-concepts to relation-concepts). REF's "Entity := Compose(Boundary, Persistence, Identity, Scope)" is a structural-realist derivation in all but name. *Gap: this is the single largest omitted literature; REF's relation-first claim lives or dies against OSR.*

**Transformation as primitive; process over substance.**
Taking change/becoming as fundamental rather than derivative is **process philosophy** — A. N. Whitehead, *Process and Reality* (1929), and Nicholas Rescher's *Process Metaphysics* (1996). Gilbert Simondon's *individuation* (entities as metastable stabilizations of a process) is almost exactly REF's "entity as stabilized relational pattern." Mark Bickhard's process ontology and the autopoiesis of Maturana & Varela (organisms as self-producing processes) are the systems-theoretic cousins. *Gap: REF's "Transformation is primitive, Entity is a stabilized pattern" is a process-ontology thesis and should be argued as one.*

**Constraint as primitive / modal / organizing.**
REF treats Constraint as carrying irreducible modal content (permitted/required/forbidden) — connecting to modal metaphysics and to **dispositional/powers** accounts (Mumford & Anjum). More pointedly, "constraint as the thing that selects which transformations persist and thereby enables organization" is the **closure-of-constraints** program in theoretical biology — Montévil & Mossio (2015) on biological organization as a closure of constraints, building on Kauffman, Rosen, and Moreno & Mossio (*Biological Autonomy*, 2015). This is the most *specific* live literature REF's Constraint primitive touches, and the most citable. *Gap: engage constraint-closure; it is REF's strongest real-science anchor.*

**Identity as invariance under constrained transformation.**
"Identity = what is preserved (an invariant) under admissible transformation within a scope" is the standard mathematical-physical account: equivalence relations and quotients; Noether's theorem (1918) tying continuous symmetry to conserved invariants; the group-theoretic view of identity/objectivity (Weyl, and again Cassirer). It contrasts with Leibniz's identity of indiscernibles and with substance theories of identity. REF's Identity is here re-deriving a well-formalized idea. *Gap: cite the invariance tradition; it makes the claim rigorous rather than novel.*

**Boundary as derived (Difference + Relation + Constraint + Scope).**
The formal theory of boundaries is **mereotopology** — Casati & Varzi, *Parts and Places* (1999) — and specifically the fiat-vs-bona-fide boundary distinction of Smith & Varzi (2000). Systems-theoretic boundary (system/environment) traces to von Bertalanffy and Luhmann. REF's "boundary is not primitive but a scoped, constrained relational distinction" is a defensible mereotopological stance. *Gap: mereotopology already formalizes this; REF should build on it, not reinvent it.*

**Emergence and Recursion.**
REF's derived Emergence/Recursion sit in a vast, contested literature: weak vs. strong emergence (Mark Bedau; David Chalmers), Timothy O'Connor on emergent properties, the levels-of-organization and downward-causation debates (Jaegwon Kim's exclusion argument is the standard challenge REF must answer). REF's "capability/explanatory-necessity" criterion for a new level is close to the epistemic/weak-emergence camp. *Gap: emergence is the most philosophically loaded term REF uses; it cannot be adopted without engaging Kim and the weak/strong distinction.*

**The enterprise itself: foundational/upper ontology and its evaluation.**
REF is, in field terms, an **upper (foundational) ontology**, and there are established ones it must be compared to: **BFO** (Basic Formal Ontology; Barry Smith and colleagues), **DOLCE** (Descriptive Ontology for Linguistic and Cognitive Engineering; Masolo, Borgo, Gangemi, Guarino, Oltramari, from the WonderWeb project), **UFO** (Unified Foundational Ontology; Giancarlo Guizzardi), plus SUMO and Cyc. Crucially, REF's "competency questions + critical gates" evaluation method closely resembles two existing methodologies: **competency questions** for ontology scoping (Grüninger & Fox, 1995) and **OntoClean** (Nicola Guarino & Christopher Welty, ~2002), which evaluates taxonomic decisions using formal meta-properties — *rigidity, identity, unity, dependence* — that map almost one-to-one onto REF's derivation concerns. *Gap: this is mandatory. An upper ontology that doesn't position against BFO/DOLCE/UFO and doesn't cite OntoClean will be desk-rejected.*

## Honest assessment and what it would take

**What REF is, positioned honestly:** an independent, engineering-flavored re-derivation that converges on a *relation/difference/process-first structural ontology* — a real, defended position in the metaphysics of science — organized with unusually disciplined governance and reproducibility machinery. The *reasoning* is competent and the *positions* are defensible. What is missing for it to be a contribution *as ontology* is not more derivation; it is scholarship and a thesis.

**To make it publishable as ontology, in order:**
1. **Position against the field.** Add explicit comparison to BFO, DOLCE, and UFO, and adopt (or explicitly critique) OntoClean's meta-property method, which your competency questions already resemble.
2. **Engage the four load-bearing traditions.** Spencer-Brown (Difference), ontic structural realism (Relation), process philosophy (Transformation), and constraint-closure (Constraint). Each is the prior owner of one of your four primitives.
3. **Answer the standard challenge to Emergence.** Address Kim's exclusion argument and the weak/strong distinction, or restrict your Emergence claim to the epistemic/weak reading.
4. **Resolve or formally characterize the open circularities.** Difference↔Relation and Transformation↔State are, by your own generator's report, unresolved. Either close them or present them as a *formal* co-foundation result (a genuine contribution if done rigorously).
5. **State a novel, defensible thesis.** The strongest candidate is *not* "these are the right primitives" (contested, un-new) but: **"a governed, deterministically-generated foundational ontology with explicit, machine-checkable unresolved-dependency accounting."** There the novelty is real — the method (see the companion methodology note), not the metaphysics. A paper whose thesis is the *governance-and-generation apparatus applied to foundational ontology*, using REF as its worked example, is publishable in a way that "here is the true primitive basis" is not.

The recommendation that runs through this whole line of work applies once more: the credible contribution lives in the disciplined *method*, and the totalizing *theory-of-everything* framing should be kept well away from it.
